The Great Float Market is the trading floor of Vanua Tafola, the largest single raft-platform in the city, a broad deck of split bamboo and balsa logs the size of a village square, painted blue and rigged with a great sail-cloth awning striped in red and yellow that shades the stalls from the midday sun and snaps and bellows in the wind. Stalls are woven-palm tables and canoe-hulls used as bins, heaped with the goods of the archipelago — smoked fish from Makapaa in hard brown ranks, obsidian blades from Motu Peleki wrapped in oiled leaf, breadfruit and taro and bananas from the garden-islands, coils of cordage, bolts of dyed bark-cloth, mother-of-pearl inlay and shell-beads, clay pots of fermented fish-paste and palm-wine, and the rare foreign goods — steel fish-hooks, copper ingots, a bolt of fine cotton, a crate of dried mangoes from across the sea. The noise is a wall of haggling in a half-dozen island dialects and the trade-pidgin that is Vanua Tafola's own tongue, punctuated by the slap of canoes tying up at the platform-edge and the cries of children diving for coins. The smell changes every few paces — fish, fruit, dye, smoke, wine, tar. A market-warden in a painted canoe poles the lanes between the stalls, settling disputes with the Trade-Master's staff, and a money-changer sits on a high stool at the center, her shell-bead scales clicking constantly.
