The lower town, where the pilgrim road meets the first terraces and the town begins. Lodging houses, tea stalls, incense shops, and sellers of prayer beads and ward-talismans line the steep main street. The air is warmer here than above — woodsmoke, cooking rice, the sweet rot of fallen cedar needles. Pilgrims rest here before the final climb; many leave offerings at the small wayside shrine at the quarter's top, where a fox spirit is said to judge the sincerity of the prayer. The townspeople are patient with the endless flow of visitors; their living depends on it. A bathhouse at the quarter's center is fed by a hot spring, and it is the last warm water a pilgrim will see before the monastery.
