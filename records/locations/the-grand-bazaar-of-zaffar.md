The Grand Bazaar of Zaffar is the greatest spice-market in the known world, a labyrinth of covered streets and vaulted caravanserais at the city's heart, its roofs a patchwork of domes and skylights that let shafts of dusty gold light fall onto the stalls below where the spice-merchants sit cross-legged among their wares. The smell is overwhelming and changes with every step — saffron, then cardamom, then pepper, then cinnamon, then the musk of frankincense and myrrh, then the sharp bite of dried ginger, then the warm sweetness of cloves — and the stalls are heaped with cones and pyramids of coloured powder, baskets of dried bark and roots, clay jars of oils and resins, and the sealed brass boxes of the most costly spices kept under the merchant's eye. Beyond the spices the bazaar sells everything — silks from Basur, gems from Naqd, Old Road fragments from Khalesh, dates from Qadira, copper, brass, coffee, tobacco, perfumes, dyes, and the strange goods the caravans bring from beyond the desert. The noise is a constant hum of haggling in a dozen tongues, the clink of scales, the rattle of coffee-cups, and the call of the muezzin. Money-changers sit at corner tables, and the binding-family agents in their dark robes move through the crowds buying and noting.
