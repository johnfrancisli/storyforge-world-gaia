A stone-walled, soot-blackened forge at the shipyard's eastern edge, set apart from the timber works by a firewall of stacked sod and open to the sea-wind on three sides for the draft, the Anchor-Iron Forge is Drekahöfn's iron-works—the forge that produces the rivets, nails, anchors, and iron-fittings that hold Hrafnland's ships together. The forge is run by a family of ship-smiths who have worked the same anvil for four generations and who supply the yard exclusively, and its master, a huge, softly-spoken man named Hákon Anchor-Hand, can judge a rivet's heat by the color of the glow and has never shipped a soft nail. The forge works bog iron and southern bar-iron, smelted in a charcoal furnace whose chimney trails a permanent grey plume over the eastern strand, and the ring of Hákon's trip-hammer on the anvil is the yard's clock, audible across the whole town. The rivets are the ship's bones—a longship holds thousands, each hand-forged, each quenched in saltwater for the frost-blue hardness the raiders trust—and the anchors are the ship's safety, great iron hooks that can hold a dragon-prowed longship in a gale. The forge smells of hot iron, coal smoke, and the sea, and its sparks fly out over the water at night like a second, lower constellation beneath the real one.
