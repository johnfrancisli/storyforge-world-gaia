The Breathing Springs are a cluster of geothermal hot pools on Motu Peleki's eastern shore where the lava-meets-the-sea, a series of stone basins filled with water that is heated from below and mixes with the warm ocean in varying proportion, so that each pool is a different temperature — from scalding to merely warm — and a different mineral colour, from milky blue to rust-orange to clear and faintly green. Steam curls off the hottest pools in slow columns that the islanders read as omens, and the air smells of hot mineral, salt, and the faint egg-tang of sulphur that the bathers no longer notice. The springs are the communal bath of the island, used for washing, healing, and the long ritual soak that follows the fire-spirit pact, and they are open to all — chief and farmer, warrior and child — with a strict peace that forbids any quarrel within the spring-basin, enforced by an attendant called the Water-Keeper who can ban anyone who breaks it. Smooth stones line the pools for seats, and the bathers scrub with pumice gathered from the shore, the island's other great export. At dusk the pools glow with reflected lava-light and the steam turns gold, and the bathers linger in the warm dark, talking low, the sea lapping just beyond the basins.
