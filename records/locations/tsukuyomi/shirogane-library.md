The rock-cut archive beneath the main temple, where the order's scrolls are kept in dry, warded chambers that smell of old paper and cedar oil and something faintly electric. Racks of bamboo and lacquered wood hold thousands of scrolls — ward-craft treatises, yokai catalogs, clan histories older than the Ashikara, martial manuals, and a sealed section the abbot will not discuss. A single monk, the Keeper of Records, holds the keys. Copies of certain scrolls are made available to scholars and shrine keepers who petition the abbot and are approved; the originals never leave the chambers. The air is still and cool and the ward-hum is strong enough that visitors sometimes hear it in their teeth. A fire here would be a catastrophe measured in centuries, and the order has built accordingly — stone walls, warded doors, and a single narrow entrance.
