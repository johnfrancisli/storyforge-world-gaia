The Great Canoe Shed is the boat-house of [[location:lani-atoll|Lani Atoll]], a long cathedral-like structure of hardwood and palm-thatch on the lagoon-shore where the archipelago's great voyaging canoes — the double-hulled vessels that carry fifty sailors and trade-goods across hundreds of leagues of open sea — are housed, repaired, and built. The shed is vast, its ridge-pole higher than a man's height doubled to fit the prows of the great canoes, and its floor is sand swept clean and strewn with wood-shavings, the smell of fresh-cut hardwood, tar, and the sweet sandalwood used for the finest prows filling the air. Three or four great canoes rest on cradles of driftwood at any time — some newly building, some being re-caulked and re-rigged, some resting between voyages — their hulls of sewn plank painted black and red with staring eye-prows at the bow to ward off sea-spirits, their twin masts stepped and their great sails of woven pandanus folded and stored in cedar chests along the wall. The master canoe-builder, a quiet man with hands scarred from a lifetime of adze-work, oversees a crew of shipwrights and apprentices, and the shed is the island's pride: a new voyaging-canoe, launched with ceremony and sung across the sea by a wayfinder from [[location:tide-hokuala|Hokuala]], is an event the whole archipelago marks. Tools — adzes, caulking-mallets, bone drills — hang on the walls, and the cordage comes fresh from [[location:tide-vanua-tafola|Vanua Tafola]]'s works.
