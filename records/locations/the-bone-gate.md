The Bone Gate is the old ceremonial entrance to the temple-city, a stone gateway carved with skulls and serpent-fangs that stands at the eastern edge of the plaza where the cleared ground meets the uncleared jungle. The gate is half-collapsed, one pillar leaning and vines choking the lintel, but the shamans have forbidden its repair, saying it marks the boundary between the living settlement and the domain of the old dead. Offerings are left here at every new moon — food, cacao, carved tokens — set on a stone ledge beneath the carved skulls, and the shamans come here to petition the ancient dead for permission before any new part of the city is reclaimed from the jungle. The air here is cooler and stiller than the plaza, the roar of the howlers seems closer, and the green wall of the rainforest presses in so near you can reach out and touch the leaves. It is said that at night the old builders walk through the gate back into their city, and the shrine-keeper on the pyramid keeps a lamp lit for them. Locals do not linger here after dark.
