On a low rise above the slipways, its ridge-pole carved with a row of miniature dragon-prows and its doorposts shaped like the stems of a longship, the Ship-Builder's Hall is the seat of Drekahöfn's rule and the social heart of the shipbuilding town—a long, high-roofed timber hall that is half longhouse, half guildhall, built to house the Ship-Builder's household, host the Shipwrights' Guild meetings, and feed the yard's crews at the launch-feasts that are the town's great occasion. The hall's central fire-pit runs two-thirds of its length, and the high seat at the western end is a carved chair whose arms are shaped from the curved stem-pieces of the first longship Gísli Iron-Rivets ever built, a trophy and a throne in one. The walls are hung with the shipwrights' tools—decorated adzes, rivet-hammers, and the great two-man crosscut saws that shape the keel-timbers—and with the painted launch-banners of every ship built in the yard, a record of the fleet that makes the hall feel less like a dwelling than like a gallery of the nation's sea-power. The air is a warm, workmanlike fug of woodsmoke, mead, pine-tar, and the sweat of shipwrights celebrating a launch, and at a launch-feast the hall is packed stem to stern with the whole town, drinking, boasting, and singing the work-chants of the yard until the high beams ring.
