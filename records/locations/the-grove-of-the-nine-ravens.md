On a windswept point above the harbor where the land falls away to the sea and the wind is a constant, keening presence, the Grove of the Nine Ravens is [[location:skaldvik|Skaldvik]]'s holiest ground—a ring of nine ancient yew trees, their branches twisted by centuries of salt wind into shapes that resemble nothing so much as the arms of drowned sailors reaching for the sky. At the grove's center stands a single runestone, waist-high and moss-dark, carved with the Nine-Raven sigil that gives the grove its name, and around it, driven into the earth at the base of each yew, are nine iron poles hung with offerings—twisted rag-charms, carved bone-tokens, locks of hair, and the occasional seidr-wand left by a volva whose magic was spent. The grove is tended by a single godhi, a grey-bearded priest named Hákon Tree-Eye, who lives in a sod hut at the grove's edge and who, it is whispered, has not left the point in thirty years. The air here smells of salt, yew resin, and the faint metallic tang of old blood from the blotr-sacrifices offered at the turning of the seasons, when the godhi bleeds a black ram and lets its blood run down the runestone to feed the land-wights. No iron is drawn in the grove, and no oath sworn there has ever been broken without the oath-breaker coming to a bad end—a reputation that keeps the grove feared and frequented in equal measure.
