The single general store, halfway up the village road, where anything a traveler or a villager needs can be found if the owner, Tama, has it or can get it. The store is a front room of packed-earth floor and cedar shelves holding rice, dried fish, lamp oil, rope, sandals, prayer cord, mountain herbs, and a few luxuries that arrive on the peddler's back once a month — sake, salt, a bolt of cloth. Tama knows everyone in the village and most of what they need. The store is also the village's post point; messages bound for the Cedar Road's next shrine are left here for the fox-spirit courier. A board by the door carries notices — weather warnings, road conditions, the occasional missing person. The store smells of dried fish and cedar and the faint sweetness of the sake jars in the back.
