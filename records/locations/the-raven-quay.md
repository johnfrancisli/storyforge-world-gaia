The Raven Quay is the great stone-and-timber harbor of Skaldvik, the busiest port in Hrafnland and the hinge on which the nation's trade with the wider world turns—a long, L-shaped stretch of piers, wharves, and warehouses where longships, knarrs, and foreign cogs jostle for berthing space and the air is a permanent cocktail of salt, tar, fish, and the sweet rot of wet timber. Stevedores in sea-boots haul bales of wool, barrels of whale oil, and crates of southern iron along the quays, while merchants shout prices in Norse, the southern tongue, and a dozen pidgins born of the trade-routes. Cranes of timber and rope swing cargo between dock and deck, and the harbor-master's tower at the quay's elbow flies a great black raven-banner that signals the port is open and the watch is set. Beneath the quays, in the salt-damp dark, smugglers move untaxed goods and stolen iron through passages that honeycomb the waterfront, and the harbor watch—six boats and a garrison of bored, bribable men—makes a show of policing what it cannot control. Every legal cargo in Hrafnland passes through the Raven Quay, and a good portion of the illegal cargo, too.
