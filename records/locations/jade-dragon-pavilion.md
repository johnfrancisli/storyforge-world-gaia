The seat of Chu power crowns a rise at the city's heart, a sprawling palace of layered jade-tiled roofs and lacquered crimson columns where the Chu warlord receives petitioners, envoys, and rivals. Inside, gilded screens depict the founding of the Chu line in scenes of river gods and dragon-riders, and the halls smell of sandalwood and the faint metallic edge of hidden blades. Eunuchs whisper court gossip in antechambers, armored guards stand at every threshold, and officials in ranked silk robes wait hours for an audience that may last only a minute. The Pavilion's gardens hide koi ponds and private alcoves where the warlord's most trusted advisors plot tax policy, marriage alliances, and the slow strangulation of rival states through trade.
