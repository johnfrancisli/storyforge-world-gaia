The Singing Grotto is the sacred heart of Lani Atoll, a sea-cave beneath the island's windward shore reached by a descending path of coral-stone steps that drops below the tide-line into a hidden chamber where the roof is pierced by a natural shaft and the walls are carved — by hands far older than the archipelago's memory — with spirals, wave-patterns, and the staring faces of sea-spirits in a style no living carver can replicate. The grotto's floor is a pool of sea-water that rises and falls with the tide, and the chamber's acoustics are its wonder: any sound made here is caught and multiplied by the carved walls and the shaft, so that a single voice becomes a choir, a chant becomes a harmony of echoes, and the wayfinding song-magic — the star-path songs that the masters of Hokuala teach — resonates here with a power and clarity found nowhere else. The island's spirit-shaman, a woman of great age who is said to have sung in the grotto since before the first chief, conducts the highest rites of the song-magic here: the binding of a master-wayfinder's spirit to the sea and the stars, the blessing of a new voyaging-canoe, and the Great Singing at the turn of the year, when the whole island gathers on the steps above and the shaman sings the archipelago into being again, her voice rising from the grotto in a cascade of harmony that is said to reach the sea-spirit and the star-spirits alike. The air is cold, wet, salt, and charged, and the carved faces seem to move in the lamplight.
