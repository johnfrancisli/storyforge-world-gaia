The Feast Ground of the Full Moon is the great gathering-place of [[location:lani-atoll|Lani Atoll]], a broad open clearing of swept sand inland from the chief's hall, ringed by breadfruit and coconut palms and dominated at its center by a great earth-oven complex — a row of stone-lined pits that can cook for hundreds — and a raised platform of coral-stone and driftwood where the drummers and chanters perform. Every full moon the whole island gathers here for the feast that is the atoll's chief ceremony and its joy: the earth-ovens are fired before dawn, the day is given to cooking and preparation, and at dusk the drums begin and the people gather, the chiefs in their feather-cloaks, the families with their children, the elders with their carved staves, and the feast is shared — roast pig and breadfruit, taro-pudding wrapped in banana leaves, fish in coconut cream, fermented breadfruit paste, and the kava and palm-wine that flow freely. The drummers on the platform play the complex polyrhythms that drive the dance, and the dancers — men and women in bark-cloth and shell-adornment — perform the story-dances of the archipelago, the voyaging, the fishing, the rising seas, the drowning of the outer islands, the finding of refuge, their bodies telling in movement what the songs tell in word. The firelight turns the clearing gold, the smoke rises through the palms, and the sound of drum and chant and laughter carries out over the lagoon. It is the heart of the archipelago's shared life, and the full-moon feasts draw visitors from every island.
