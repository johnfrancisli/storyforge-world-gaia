The Cordage Works is the craft-quarter of [[location:tide-vanua-tafola|Vanua Tafola]], a cluster of smaller raft-platforms moored in a quieter corner of the lagoon where the rope-makers, sail-makers, and net-weavers have their workshops, chosen for the calm water that lets them lay out long lines and dry sails without the current carrying them away. The sound is constant — the rhythmic twist of drop-spindles, the creak of rope-walks where three-strand cordage is laid up by teams walking backwards the length of a platform, and the soft murmur of women sewing sail-cloth of woven pandanus-leaf on bone needles. Coconut fibre and hibiscus bark are processed in shallow vats of sea-water that soften the fibres, and the air smells of wet husk, salt, and the faint tannin of the bark. The cordage made here — from fine fishing-line to hawser-thick mooring-rope — is the best in the archipelago and is traded to every island and to the wayfinder-school at [[location:tide-hokuala|Hokuala]], and the sails, painted with the eye-prows and wave-patterns of the voyaging canoes, are prized by every captain. The cordage-master, a barrel-chested man with hands like leather, oversees quality and lays the long rope-walks himself, and his stamp — a distinctive triple-knot — marks every coil that leaves the works.
