Along the fast-flowing channel cut from the White Crane River, a row of watermills turns day and night, their great wooden wheels creaking and splashing as they grind grain into flour, press oil from seeds, and full cloth for the textile trade. The district is a working quarter of millers, bakers, and oil-pressers whose homes and workshops cluster around each mill, connected by narrow paths worn smooth by decades of foot traffic. The air smells of fresh flour, warm bread, and the mineral tang of river water channeled through stone races. Apprentices haul sacks of grain up to the mill hoppers while millers in flour-dusted aprons adjust the grindstones by ear, listening for the perfect pitch that signals the right fineness. The district feeds Sanyuan and exports flour by barge to every city on the river.
