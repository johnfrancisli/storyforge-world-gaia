Where the Silver Serpent River bends through Chudu, the canal docks form a forest of masts, barges, and sampans lashed together in tiers that stretch half a mile along the waterline. Stevedores with sun-darkened skin haul crates of rice and salt up slick stone ramps, their chants rising above the creak of rope and the splash of oars. The smell is a potent mix of river water, tar, fish, and the sweat of a thousand laborers. Customs officials in drab grey robes board every vessel to assess tariffs, while smugglers slip contraband beneath false hull planks. River pirates are said to operate from the downstream islands, and the docks never truly sleep—lanterns burn all night as cargo moves under cover of darkness.
