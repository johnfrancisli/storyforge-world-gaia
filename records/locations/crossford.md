At the crossroads of the King's Road and the Old Salt Route, where a shallow stone ford crosses the Willowmere Creek, the trade hub of Crossford has grown from a single coaching inn into a sprawling, restless town of merchants, wagoners, and fortune-seekers in barely two generations. The streets are wide and rutted, lined with inns, stables, and storehouses whose signs shout in a babel of languages, and the constant rumble of wagon wheels and clop of hooves gives the whole place the feel of a river in flood. Canvas awnings shade open-air markets where spices from the southern coasts, wool from the highland flocks, and enchanted trinkets from [[location:val-elfshade|Elfshade]] change hands amid a din of haggling, laughter, and the occasional shriek of a pickpocket caught by the Watch. The smell of roasting chestnuts, horse dung, and the distinctive pepper-and-clove scent of southern spices mingles over everything. Crossford has no ancient bloodlines or grand architecture—its oldest building is a forty-year-old inn—but it has coin, energy, and a population that doubles during the seasonal fairs. The Merchant Guild's chapterhouse here is the largest outside [[location:val-rivenspire|Rivenspire]], and its Master is said to be richer than half the lords of the realm.
