The shrine at the north gate, small and smoky and far more important than its size suggests. A single priest, an old man named Jiro who lost his left hand to a frost-oni twenty years ago, tends the gate wardstones and the shrine's blue fires that burn at the stockade threshold in all weather. The wardstones here are heavier, cruder, and more frequently replaced than those of the lowland cities — the northern yokai hit harder, and the stones crack under the strain. Jiro keeps a rack of spares and recharges them on a forge he built into the shrine's back wall. Offerings are left by soldiers going on patrol: sake, salt, a lock of hair. Some come back to collect the hair. Many do not. The shrine is the thin line between the town and what lives in the deep mountains, and everyone in [[location:tsu-kitagoe|Kitagoe]] knows it.
