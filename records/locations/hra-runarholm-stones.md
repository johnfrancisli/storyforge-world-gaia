At the moor's heart, where the frost-heave has pushed the earth into a low, uneven dome and the moss grows thick and grey-green over stone older than memory, the Ring of the First Runes is Rúnarhólm's holiest ground and the reason the village exists at all—a circle of twenty-four standing stones, each taller than a man, each carved from top to bottom with runes so old that even the Master Carver admits he does not read them all. The stones are not uniform: some are rough-hewn granite, dark with lichen; others are pale, almost white, a stone found nowhere else in Hrafnland and said to have been carried by the first rune-carvers from a quarry now lost under the ice. The runes themselves are cut deep, recut every generation by the Rune-Guild to keep the frost from wearing them smooth, and at dawn and dusk the carvers walk the ring in slow procession, chanting the rune-names in a droning harmony that the villagers say holds the frost giants in their sleep beneath the moor. The air in the ring is still, colder than the surrounding moor, and charged with a faint, persistent hum that sets the teeth aching and the small hairs of the arms rising—a hum the carvers call 'the stone-singing' and that no seidr-woman has fully explained. No one stands in the ring's center after dark, not even the Master Carver, for the stones are said to wake when the light is gone, and what they wake as is a thing the old sagas do not name.
