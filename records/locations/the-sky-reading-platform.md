The Sky-Reading Platform is the night-observatory of [[location:tide-hokuala|Hokuala]], a raised platform of hardwood poles and split bamboo built on the island's eastern shore where the reef falls away into deep water and the horizon is dark and clear, used by the masters and senior students for the night-watches that are the final and most demanding phase of wayfinder training. The platform is open to the sky, its only shelter a low wind-screen of woven palm, and its floor is strewn with woven mats where the watchers lie on their backs and read the stars — the rising and setting of the guide-stars, the slow arc of the sea-stars that mark the seasons, the phosphorescent wakes of the deep-water currents that a master can read as a second compass in the dark sea below. The air at night is cold for the tropics, salt and clear, and the silence is deep, broken only by the lap of the reef and the low murmur of a master naming a star to a student or correcting a sighting. A small clay fire-pot on the platform's edge holds a smouldering ember for warmth and for the green tea brewed through the long watches, and the students learn here to read the sky without instruments — by eye, memory, and the song-magic that binds the star-paths into a single mental map of the voyaging-world. It is here, on a clear night at the turn of the season, that a graduating student makes their vigil — a night alone on the platform, reading every star and swell, to prove they are ready for their first true voyage.
