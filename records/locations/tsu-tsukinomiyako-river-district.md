The commercial heart of the capital, built along the Moon River where it curves through the low town. Warehouses, counting houses, and merchant mansions of dark timber and white plaster line the embankment, their eaves nearly touching across the narrower lanes. The smell of river water, incense from the district shrine, and the green tea served in every counting house. This is where the nation's wealth is counted and moved — rice tithes, silk, silver from the western mines, all flowing through the river district's books. The merchant guilds are powerful here, and their relationship with the shogunate's magistrates is a careful balance of favors and tariffs. The river bridges are hung with lanterns at night, and the district is beautiful in a way that makes it easy to forget that fortunes are destroyed here as calmly as they are made.
