The old shrine at the top of the village's single road, where the torii stands mossy and the cedar shade is thickest. A small sanctuary — one hall, one altar, one wardstone that anchors this stretch of the Cedar Road's protection. The shrine keeper, Miko Sora, lives in the attached cottage and has maintained the ward for longer than the village's memory of anyone else doing so. The wardstone is modest compared to the capital's, but it is well-tended, and the fox that watches from the torii at dusk is, by village reputation, the same fox that has watched for decades. Pilgrims and travelers leave offerings here before continuing up or down the Cedar Road. The shrine smells of cedar, moss, and the faint ozone of a wardstone under steady care. Shamisen music drifts down from somewhere above, and no one is entirely sure who plays it.
