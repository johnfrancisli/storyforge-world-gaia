The Guest Floats are the lodging-quarter of [[location:tide-vanua-tafola|Vanua Tafola]], a cluster of smaller raft-platforms moored at the lagoon's downwind edge where foreign traders, visiting islanders, and the crews of trading-canoes stay while they do business in the city. The floats are simple — low thatched shelters of palm-leaf on bamboo decks, each sleeping six to ten on woven mats, with a shared cook-platform and a freshwater butt refilled daily from the islets' springs — but they are clean and dry and the innkeeper, a stout woman called the Float-Mother, keeps a strict order that makes the guest-quarters the safest place in the city after dark. The floats rock gently with the swell, and sleepers who are not island-born often wake in the night disoriented by the motion, the lap of water beneath them, and the creak of the mooring-lines. A chandler's stall on the central float sells provisions, tar, sail-cloth, and dried fish to departing crews, and a story-teller works the cook-platform in the evenings, trading tales of distant islands and the open sea for food and wine. The air smells of thatch, cooking fish, lamp-oil, and the salt-and-tar of a hundred sailors' gear. The Float-Mother collects lodging fees, settles the inevitable quarrels, and reports trouble to the float-guard.
