The Sunset Gate is the great western gate of Khalesh, the city's opening onto the gravel plain and the sand-sea beyond, built into a gap in the Old Road's old stone wall that the first settlers widened and roofed with a great lintel of black stone carved with the worn figures of the old empire's gods. The gate is the city's trade-mouth and its danger — through it come the relic-diggers with their finds, the caravans with their goods, and the desert nomads with their wool, but beyond it lies the deep sand-sea where the Old Road's fragments are found and where men are lost to the sand and the storm and the things the desert keeps. The gate is guarded by the Warden's gate-watch, who check the diggers' licenses and the caravans' loads and who keep the great horn that is sounded at the closing of the gate at dusk and at the sighting of a sandstorm on the horizon. Just inside the gate stands the Marker Stone, a great old-road pillar re-erected by the first Wardens, carved with the distances to the known sites of the Old Road fragments, and at its base the diggers leave offerings of stone and water before they set out. The wind at the gate is constant and carries the dry smell of the deep sand and, sometimes, the faint ringing sound that the old metal tokens are said to make when the wind blows across them in the desert.
