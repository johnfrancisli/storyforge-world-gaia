The Crossroads Inn is the oldest building in Crossford and the reason the town exists at all—a sprawling, three-story timber-and-stone hostelry that stands at the literal intersection of the King's Road and the Old Salt Route, its sign a painted crossroads visible from every approach. The inn has grown by accretion over forty years, each owner adding a wing, a stable, a cellar, or a second floor, until it is now a labyrinth of corridors, sudden stairs, and rooms that open onto unexpected courtyards. The common room is the largest in the region, a hall of dark wood and roaring hearths that seats two hundred, and at the height of the seasonal fairs it is standing-room-only, filled with merchants, wagoners, guards, and travelers of every race and nation. The air is a thick, warm fug of pipe smoke, ale, horse-leather, and the rich smell of the inn's famous crossroads stew—a thick, spicy concoction that changes daily depending on what the kitchen has. The innkeeper, a jovial, red-faced halfling named Broddy Fairhand, knows every road, every merchant, and every rumor between Crownhaven and the southern coast, and his inn is the unofficial post office, news exchange, and hiring hall of the town. A room at the Crossroads Inn during fair season is worth more than a room at the Gilded Drake, and booking one requires either advance planning or a very persuasive purse.
