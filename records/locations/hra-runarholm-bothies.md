The interconnected ring of low, windowless stone bothies that forms the village proper is less a settlement than a single building shaped like a broken ring around the standing stones—each bothy sharing a wall with its neighbors, each roofed with turf grown over with frost-heather, and each connected to the next by a low, drafty passage that lets a carver walk the whole village without stepping outside. The bothies are the homes and workshops of the Rune-Guild's members, and every interior surface—doorposts, lintels, roof-beams, even the stone headrests of the sleeping-benches—is carved with runes: protective wards above the doors, lineage-records on the bed-posts, weather-prophecies on the hearth-lintels, and the names of the dead on the memorial-stones set into the floors. The air is a permanent, dry, mineral scent of fresh-cut stone and the faint ozone of active seidr, and the sound of chisel on stone is the village's constant, quiet music, audible through the shared walls at all hours. Each bothy has a single hearth, a single sleeping-bench, and a work-table strewn with chisels, rune-punches, and the small, meticulous tools of the carver's craft, and the carvers themselves—men and women of quiet, focused intensity—work in a concentration so deep that a visitor can stand in the doorway unremarked for a quarter-hour before being noticed.
