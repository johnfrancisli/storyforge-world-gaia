The Training Canoe House is the boat-shed of [[location:tide-hokuala|Hokuala]], a long open-fronted hall of hardwood and palm-thatch on the lagoon-shore where the training-canoes are kept — small outriggers built light for single-student sailing, each fitted with a miniature sail and a star-compass mark painted on the bow for the student to orient by. The canoes are racked on driftwood stands in rows, their hulls scraped and re-painted each season, and the shed smells of wood-shavings, fresh paint, tar, and the salt-damp of the lagoon. A boat-master, a retired wayfinder who lost his sea-legs to age but not his eye, builds and maintains the training-canoes here with two apprentices, and he also oversees the practical sailing-exercises that are the core of the training: each student must solo a training-canoe from the lagoon to a marked point beyond the reef and back, by star and swell alone, before they are allowed near a true voyaging-canoe. The shed's back wall holds the graduated staffs of every wayfinder trained here, each carved with their name and graduating voyage, a record stretching back generations, and the students touch the wall for luck before each solo exercise. The lagoon is visible through the open front, its water bright and shallow, the reef-edge marked by the line of breaking white where the training-ground begins.
