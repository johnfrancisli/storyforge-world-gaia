The Coral House is the seat of the Port-Warden of Basur, a fortified palace of coral-rock and lime at the headland's summit, its walls pale pink and pocked with the holes of the coral from which it is built, its flat roof crowned with a high watch-tower from which the Warden can see the whole harbour, the strait, and the salt-flats to the north. Within, the halls are paved in dark stone brought as ballast from foreign ships and hung with the flags and captured banners of the merchant houses and the pirate-fleets the Warden's guards have broken, and the air smells of salt, ink, and the strong coffee the Warden drinks through the long watches. The Warden's seat is a heavy chair of ship's timber behind a great desk of driftwood-fig, piled with the customs-ledgers, harbour-tallies, and the sealed letters from the Sheikh of Zaffar and the foreign consuls. A gallery of windows looks out over the harbour so that the Warden can watch the ships come and go as he works. Guards in salt-bleached tunics with curved swords stand at the doors, and a harbour-signal station on the roof flies flags to direct traffic and to warn of storms or strange sails.
