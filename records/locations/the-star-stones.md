The Star Stones are the great navigation-circle of Hokuala, a permanent ring of standing stones set in the sand of the island's highest point — a low dune barely above the palm-tops but the only spot on the flat atoll with an unbroken view of the horizon in every direction. Thirty-two stones, each waist-high and carved with the face of its star, stand in a circle around a central stone that marks the navigator's stance, and the sand between them is raked smooth each evening so that the master can lay out the swell-patterns and current-directions in sticks and shells, teaching the students to read the whole ocean from a single point. The stones are black basalt brought from Motu Peleki and Makapaa, each hauled across the sea and set by the joint labor of the archipelago's islands as an offering to the wayfinder's art, and they are sacred — touched only by masters and graduating students, who lay their hands on their birth-star stone as they receive their navigator's staff. By night the circle is the island's observatory, the masters and senior students standing within it reading the stars through the carved sighting-grooves on each stone, their voices low as they confirm the star-paths and track the slow drift of the guide-stars through the seasons. The wind is constant here at the dune-top, and the air smells of salt and sand and the cold clarity of the open sky.
