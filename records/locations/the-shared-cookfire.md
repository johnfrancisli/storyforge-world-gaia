The Shared Cookfire is the communal kitchen and gathering-place of [[location:tide-motu-lotu|Motu Lotu]], a broad sand clearing at the camp's center ringed with driftwood logs for seats and dominated by a row of earth-ovens — pits lined with heated stones and banana leaves — where the camp's daily meals are cooked collectively, each household contributing fish or produce to a shared pot and receiving back a share scaled to its size. The cookfire is tended by a rotation of camp-wide volunteers overseen by the Fire-Mother, a formidable elder who controls the ovens and the food-ration with an exact sense of fairness, and the smell of roasting fish, breadfruit, and taro hangs over the clearing from dawn to dusk, the smoke drifting through the palms in blue threads. The Shared Cookfire is also the camp's social heart — the place where news is exchanged, where the Camp-Council makes announcements, where the elders tell the old stories of the drowned islands in the evenings so that the children remember, and where the musicians — refugees who brought their instruments and their songs — play the nose-flute and the slit-drum while the camp sings the songs of a dozen islands, some mournful, some joyful, all carrying the weight of what was lost. At night the firelight turns the clearing amber, and the gathered camp — many islands, many clans, one people now — eats together and the sound of their voices is like the sea.
