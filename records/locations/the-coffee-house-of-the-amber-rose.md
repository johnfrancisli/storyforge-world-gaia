The Coffee House of the Amber Rose is the most famous of [[location:zaffar|Zaffar]]'s many coffee-houses, a low vaulted hall of old brick opening onto a shaded garden of orange trees and a small fountain, where the spice-merchants, caravan-masters, binding-family agents, and foreign traders gather to drink coffee, smoke the long water-pipes, and conduct the business that the bazaar is too loud and the palace too formal for. The coffee is brewed strong and bitter in brass pots over charcoal and served in small handleless cups with dates, and the smoke of the rose-and-tobacco pipes hangs in blue layers under the vaulted ceiling, scenting the whole place with a heavy sweet fog. Low divans line the walls and the centre is given over to small tables where men sit playing chess and backgammon, reading poems to each other, or haggling in low voices over a brass tray of samples. A blind storyteller in the corner recounts the old tales of the djinn and the lost empires for coins, and a boy circulates with a brass tray of sweet pastries. The Amber Rose is where the real power of [[location:zaffar|Zaffar]] is brokered — alliances, prices, binding-contracts, and betrayals — and a sharp visitor can learn more here in an evening than in a week at the bazaar.
