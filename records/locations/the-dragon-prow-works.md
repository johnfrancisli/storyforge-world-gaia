A long, open-fronted shed at the shipyard's western end, where the wind carries off the shavings and the light is good for fine carving, the Dragon-Prow Works is the workshop of [[location:hra-drekahofn|Drekahöfn]]'s carvers—the specialists who shape the figureheads that give [[location:hrafnland|Hrafnland]]'s longships their name and their terror. The shed is strewn with wood-shavings to the ankle, its benches lined with half-finished prows in pine and oak: dragons with gilded teeth, ravens with spread wings, serpents coiled to strike, and the occasional wolf's-head for a jarl's personal ship. The master carver, a lean, quiet, long-fingered man named Leif Dragon-Cut, can spend a month on a single prow and has never shipped one he did not consider worthy, and his work commands a price second only to the hull itself. The air smells of fresh-cut pine, pine-tar, and the faint mineral scent of the gilding-leaf that the carvers apply to teeth, eyes, and wing-tips, and the shed's back wall is hung with the carved eyes—gilded, painted, or set with amber—that are the last piece fitted before a ship is launched. A ship's prow is its luck, the raiders say, and a ship that puts to sea with a botched prow is a ship that will not come home, so the carvers are honored as highly as the shipwrights and paid in silver, iron, and a share of the raid-spoil when the ship returns.
