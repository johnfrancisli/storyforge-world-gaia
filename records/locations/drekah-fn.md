At the wide mouth of the Drekafjord where the greatest waterway of [[location:hrafnland|Hrafnland]] pours into the whale-road, Drekahöfn is the shipbuilding town of the nation—a long, roaring strand of boathouses, slipways, and tar-pits where the bones of [[location:hrafnland|Hrafnland]]'s pride are shaped from oak, pine, and iron. The town straggles along a shelving beach of grey sand and driftwood, its timber yards stacked with cured planks that smell of pine resin and pitch, and the air is thick with the ring of adzes, the hiss of hot rivets quenched in seawater, and the bellowed work-chants of crews bending planks to frames. Longships in every stage of completion lean against the shore like sleeping dragons: bare keels propped on chocks, half-planked skeletons lashed with withes, and finished vessels freshly tarred and gleaming, their dragon-prows carved and painted and waiting for the sea. The shipwrights are a proud, secretive craft-guild who guard the lines and curves of their hulls as a smith guards his steel, and their master, the Ship-Builder Gísli Iron-Rivet, can judge a hull's speed from the hang of its strakes and has never launched a slow ship. Drekahöfn builds the warships that carry [[location:hrafnland|Hrafnland]]'s raiders and the knarrs that carry its traders, and every keel laid on its shore is a promise that the nation's reach will not shrink.
