A clearing in the upper pines above the monastery, where the tengu who roost in the White Crane range come to teach — or to test. The ground is packed earth and old needles, ringed by cedar so tall the sky is a narrow strip above. Wooden posts stand in a circle, scarred by centuries of sword cuts. The tengu teach a sword style that has no name and no written form; it is learned in the body or not at all. They accept students by judgment alone — some are turned away at the treeline, some are tested for a season and released, and a very few are taught. A tengu lesson leaves bruises that heal slowly and insights that do not heal at all. The monks do not come here. The tengu do not come to the monastery. The arrangement is old and understood.
