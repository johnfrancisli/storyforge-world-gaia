The great monastery at the summit — six halls of dark timber and sweeping eaves, a gold-leafed main temple, a library built into the rock of the peak itself, and a training ground of packed earth where monks and tengu practice together at dawn. The abbot, an ancient monk named Tesshin, is said to have studied under a tengu master and to be older than the current shogun's grandfather. The library holds scrolls on ward-craft, yokai lore, and martial philosophy that exist nowhere else; access is granted by the abbot alone, and he grants it rarely. The main temple's altar holds a shard of wardstone that hums with a frequency that makes the teeth ache. The air here is very still. Time moves differently at the top of the mountain, and the monks will tell you so without smiling.
