The heart of Drekahöfn, a wide, shelving stretch of grey sand and timber shoring where the slipways run down to the waterline and the air rings with the adze and the rivet-hammer, the Longkeel Shipyard is where Hrafnland's longships are born—the greatest shipbuilding yard in the nation and the source of the fleet that carries the raiders to the whale-road and the traders to the south. The yard holds six slipways, each a cradle of timber shoring and wedge-blocks where a hull is built keel-up and then levered, with the whole town's muscle, to be rolled on logs into the sea. A keel-laying draws every shipwright in the yard, and the laying of a warship's keel is a rite overseen by the Ship-Builder himself, who drives the first rivet with a silver hammer that is then locked away until the next. The yard's timber yards, stacked with cured pine and oak planks that smell of resin and pitch, flank the slipways, and the tar-pits—a row of smoking, bubbling cauldrons where hull-planks are dipped before fitting—trail a permanent, acrid haze that the town breathes without noticing. The shipwrights work in crews of six to a slipway, each crew's master a grey-bearded veteran who knows every line of the hull by touch, and the yard's output—three to five longships a year, and twice as many knarrs—is the measure of Hrafnland's reach.
