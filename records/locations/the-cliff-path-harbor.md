The Cliff-Path Harbor is the only entry to [[location:tide-makapaa|Makapaa]], a sea-cave at the island's western base that floods at high tide and exposes a narrow shelf of wet rock at low tide, where the fishing canoes are hauled up and lashed to iron rings hammered into the cave-wall by generations of fishermen. At low tide the cave is a dim echoing vault of grey stone, its floor slippery with kelp and the pooled catch-water, lit by the white open mouth where the waves still roll in, and the canoes — narrow outriggers painted black and red with staring eye-prows to ward off sea-spirits — lie in rows on the shelf, their hulls crusted with salt. A switchback path of steps cut into the rock climbs from the cave's back to the village above, past a shrine-mark where each fisherman taps his forehead and murmurs a prayer to the sea before the climb. At high tide the cave fills and the canoes must be poled out through the breaking surf, a skilled and dangerous job done by the younger men, and the cave-mouth roars with white water. The air is of salt, wet kelp, and tar from the canoe-caulking, and the echoes make every voice seem doubled. A harbor-warden, the Cave-Keeper, logs each canoe in and out on a notched stick.
