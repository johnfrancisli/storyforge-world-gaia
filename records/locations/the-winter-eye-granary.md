Dug into the north-facing slope of the fjord's wall, where the frozen ground keeps the interior cold even in the height of summer, the Winter-Eye Granary is [[location:hra-eyrby|Eyrby]]'s storehouse against famine—a half-buried complex of stone-walled, turf-roofed chambers linked by low, drafty passages, holding enough grain, smoked meat, and seed-stock to feed [[location:hrafnland|Hrafnland]]'s south through two failed harvests. The granary's entrance is a single low door of iron-bound oak, flanked by two carved stone eyes—the 'winter-eyes' that give the place its name and that, the farmers believe, watch for the coming of the Fimbul-winter and the frost giants' last march. Inside, the air is cold, dry, and still, smelling of dust, dry grain, and the faint resin of the pine planks that line the storage bins. Sacks of barley, bins of seed-grain, and hanging rows of smoked mutton and salted fish are stacked in careful, inventory-ordered rows, and a granary-warden—a dour, precise man named Ólafur Dry-Hand—keeps a tally-board that is said to be more accurate than the jarl's own. The Winter-Eye is opened only by the jarl's seal and the warden's key, together, and in the lean years—when the frost comes early or the raiders burn the fields—what comes out of the Winter-Eye is the difference between [[location:hra-eyrby|Eyrby]] surviving and [[location:hra-eyrby|Eyrby]] becoming a saga of warning.
