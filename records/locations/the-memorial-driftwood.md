The Memorial Driftwood is the sacred place of [[location:tide-motu-lotu|Motu Lotu]], a great stand of driftwood logs — some as thick as a man's body, bleached bone-white by sun and salt — set upright in the sand at the camp's windward edge where the beach faces the open sea and the drowned islands beyond. Each log is carved with the name of a lost island, the date of its drowning, and the names of the dead who did not escape, the carving done by survivors with shell-adzes and obsidian blades, the letters filled with charcoal and resin so they stand black against the white wood. New logs are added as new islands fall, and the stand has grown from a handful to a small forest of pale pillars that creak and sway in the wind, hung with offerings of flowers, shell-leis, and the small carved figures of the ancestors the drowned islands honored. At every new moon the camp gathers here in silence as the sea-shaman — a refugee from the first drowned atoll — chants the names of the lost islands and pours an offering of fresh water and palm-wine into the sea, asking the sea-spirit to remember the dead and to spare the living a little longer. The air at the Memorial is always quieter than the rest of the camp, the wind hushed by the logs, and the sand at their base is strewn with the faded leis of past ceremonies. It is the camp's most sacred ground, and no one passes it without pausing, and no child plays here.
