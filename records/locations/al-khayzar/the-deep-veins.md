The Deep Veins are the great mine-galleries of Naqd, the heart of the mountain's wealth and its danger, a labyrinth of tunnels driven deep into the red sandstone following the veins of turquoise, lapis, and beryl that the old surveyors traced centuries ago. The mine-heads yawn at the defile's base, great dark mouths shored with timber and dripping with mine-water, and from them the galleries descend in steps and slopes into the mountain, lit by clay oil-lamps set in wall-niches and by the miners' own hand-lamps, the air growing hotter and staler and more metallic with every step down. The miners work in teams — the cutters with their pick-axes, the haulers with their baskets, the timber-men shoring the weak rock — and they are a hard and quiet breed, marked by the stone-dust in their lungs and the scars of cave-ins, paid in stone-shares and loyal to their House. The veins are narrow and follow the rock's own line, so the galleries twist and dip and sometimes break into vast natural caverns hung with crystals, or into old workings of a forgotten people whose tools are sometimes found in the dark. The mine-water is pumped by great wooden screws turned by mules at the surface, and the air is moved by great leather bellows. The smell is of stone, oil, sweat, and the iron-tang of the deep.
