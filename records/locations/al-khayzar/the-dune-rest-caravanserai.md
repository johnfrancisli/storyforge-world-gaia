The Dune Rest is the caravanserai of Qadira, set at the desert edge of the tent-ring where the sand begins, a great square enclosure of mud-brick with a single tall gate facing the deep desert, its walls buttressed against the drifting sand and its interior a vast yard of packed earth where the caravans assemble before the long roads and rest after them. The arcades hold stables for the camels and storerooms for the loads, and the upper floor has rows of small cells for the drivers and guards, each with a window of carved lattice that catches the night breeze. At the centre of the yard stands the Well of the Rest, a deep stone well whose water is sweeter than the lake's and is given freely to any traveller — a tradition the oasis families maintain as a point of honour. At dusk the yard fills with the fires of the caravan-clans cooking their evening meal, the smell of rice and mutton and coffee rising into the cool air, and the drivers sit in circles telling road-stories and arguing over the news from the far cities. A tall wooden post by the gate bears the carved marks of every clan that has passed through, some centuries old.
