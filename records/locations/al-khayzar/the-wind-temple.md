The Wind-Temple is the shrine of Khalesh, a roofless circle of old-stone pillars on a rise just within the city wall, open to the sky and the wind, dedicated to the desert-wind and the djinn of the storm, for at the desert-edge the wind is the power that gives and takes life — it brings the sand that buries and the rain that is rare and precious, and it strips the flesh from the bones of the unwary. The temple is the oldest structure in Khalesh, some of its pillars carved with the old empire's script and some uncarved and weather-smooth, and within the circle the wind-shaman — a wild-eyed man wrapped in dust-coloured wool — keeps the wind-offerings and reads the wind's intent in the way the sand moves across the circle's floor and the way the prayer-ribbons tied to the pillars flutter and tear. The people of Khalesh come here before a journey into the sand-sea, tying a ribbon to a pillar and pouring a handful of sand into the wind as an offering, and the diggers will not set out without the shaman's reading. At the great storms the shaman stands alone in the circle and chants to the wind, and the city watches from its walls, and it is said that the wind has never harmed Khalesh while the shaman stood in the circle. The air is never still here and smells of dust and the dry electric smell of the coming storm.
