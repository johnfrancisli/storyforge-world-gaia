On a level, grass-grown mound at the southern edge of town, where the windbreak of birch opens to the sky and the view sweeps the whole fjord-valley, the Thing-Mound of Eyrby is the open-air assembly-place where the free farmers of the south gather to make law, settle blood-feuds, and elect their jarl—a flat-topped barrow, ancient and grassed-over, ringed by a circle of standing boundary-stones each carved with the mark of a founding family. At the mound's center, a single flat stone—the Law-Stone—serves as the speaker's platform, and around it the farmers sit on the grass in concentric rings, their weapons stacked at the ring's edge by ancient custom, for no iron is drawn at the Thing. The Law-Speaker, a memory-trained elder who knows the farming-custom by heart, recites the law at the opening of each assembly, and his voice carries across the mound in the still air of the late summer morning. The mound is used only for the Thing and for the harvest-home blessing, and for the rest of the year it is left to the grass and the land-wights, but every farmer in Eyrby knows its contours as well as his own field, for the law made here is the law he lives by.
