The Still Water Lodge is the shaman's lodge of Iara, set apart on a small platform of black wood moored in a quiet backwater off the main river, screened by a grove of standing dead trees hung with moss and offerings so that it is half-hidden from the city. The lodge is a low round hut of woven reeds and dark wood, its entrance a low arch draped with a curtain of shells and bone that clatters in the wind. Inside, the floor is covered with woven mats and a shallow sand-pit holds the ritual fire, its smoke guided out through a reed tube. The walls are hung with spirit-masks of carved and painted wood — anaconda, caiman, river-dolphin, jaguar — each the face of a spirit-pact held by the lodge. The chief shaman, a scarred and quiet man, conducts the binding of citizens to their totem spirits here, through nights of fasting, chanting, and the drinking of the sacred vine-brew, and he also heals the sick, reads omens in the river's flow, and blesses the fishing fleet and the war-canoes. The air is thick with the smell of mapacho smoke, copal, the bitter vine-brew, and the wet green smell of the backwater. Only the initiated enter freely; others come in need and leave when their business is done.
