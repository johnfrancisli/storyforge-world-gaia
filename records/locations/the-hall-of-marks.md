The Hall of Marks is the loremasters' workshop and archive, set in a long rectangular temple-building at the plaza's edge whose roof has been restored with new thatch over the original stone walls. Inside, long tables of salvaged limestone slabs are covered with bark-paper, charcoal sketches, and clay tablets being painstakingly cleaned and catalogued. The loremasters work here by the light of oil-lamps and shafts of sun from gaps in the thatch, transcribing the old carvings, debating their meaning, and teaching the young apprentices who sit on the floor copying glyphs onto bark. Shelves along the walls hold restored relics — jade ear-plugs, a feathered mask, obsidian blades, painted pots — and fragments too broken to identify are sorted into baskets. The air smells of dust, lamp-oil, charcoal, and the sharp resin used to preserve fragile carvings. It is quiet work, broken by the slow drip of water through the roof in the rains and the low murmur of scholarly argument. The head loremaster, a grey-bearded man with ink-stained fingers, keeps a strict silence-rule during transcription hours.
