The Well of Mirrors is the sacred shrine of Qadira, set apart in a grove of ancient tamarisks at the lake's deep end, where the water is clearest and coldest. It is both a well and a temple — a stone shaft lined with mirrors of polished obsidian and brass set into the masonry, so that when one looks down into the well one sees not only the water but a thousand reflections of the sky, the trees, and one's own face, said to be the faces of the djinn who dwell in the water and answer those who come to the well with offerings and true questions. The shrine is tended by the Water-Mystic, a veiled woman who has sworn never to leave the grove, who interprets the visions seen in the well and pours the offerings of milk, rosewater, and date-wine that the suppliants bring. The air is cool and still and smells of water, tamarisk, and the faint sweetness of the offerings. The people of Qadira come here before a long journey to ask the water-djinn for safe passage, and the caravan-masters will not set out without the Mystic's blessing. It is said that the well shows the truth of a question only to those who ask in earnest, and lies to all others, and that to throw a lie into the well is to bring the djinn's anger on the whole oasis.
