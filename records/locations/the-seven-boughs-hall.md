The Seven Boughs Hall is the council chamber of Alta Canopy, built where the seven great trees converge on a single massive platform of interlocking heartwood, wider than a river barge. The roof is a living canopy left intact overhead, its leaves filtering the light into green-gold shafts that move across the floor like slow water. Seven carved seats of dark ironwood ring a central fire-pit that never fully goes out — its smoke rises through a gap left in the leaves above, and its embers are kept alive with sacred woods brought up from the forest floor by sworn climbers. Feathers, shrunken heads, and spirit-masks hang from the rafters, turning gently. The air smells of old smoke, copal incense, and the faint sweetness of fermented cassava. Petitioners wait on a lower platform and are called up one at a time by a herald who beats a hollow log drum. When the Seven are in session the hall hums with low deliberation; when they are not, it is kept by silent attendants who tend the fire and ward the masks.
