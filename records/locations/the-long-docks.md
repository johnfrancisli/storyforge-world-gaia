The Long Docks are the harbour district of Iara, a line of timber wharves and landing-stages running along the city's downstream edge where the river widens, built on pilings and extending out into the current so that vessels of any size can tie up. The docks are the busiest part of the city at all hours — war-canoes of the river-guard tying up from patrol, trading canoes and barges arriving from the interior with jungle goods, larger dhows coming up from Tidal Reach with salt and sea-fish, and the painted pleasure-canoes of the wealthy gliding past. Stevedores haul cargo up the ramps, boat-builders caulk and tar hulls on the staging-blocks, and rope-makers spin cordage on the shore. A chandler sells tar, sail-cloth, fish-hooks, and dried provisions from a stilt-shed at the head of the docks. The water is busy with small ferries poling people across the river, and children dive from the wharf-ends for coins thrown by sailors. The air smells of tar, hemp, river-mud, fish, and the smoke of the chandler's tar-pot. A dock-master in a canoe with a painted prow directs traffic and collects berthing fees.
