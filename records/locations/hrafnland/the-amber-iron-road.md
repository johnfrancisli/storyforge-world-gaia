The single track that climbs the moor from the southern lowlands, a causeway of flat stones laid over the bog where the frost-heave is worst, the Amber-Iron Road is [[location:hra-runarholm|Rúnarhólm]]'s only link to the wider world and the route by which the village's rune-work goes out and its payment comes in. The road is narrow, winding, and impassable for carts—everything that moves along it moves on a man's back or a pack-pony's—and it is marked at every turning by a rune-stone, a waist-high way-marker carved with a directional rune and the distance to the next stone, which the carvers maintain as part of their sacred duty. At the road's moor-end, where it meets the bothy-ring, a single open-faced trading-stone—a flat-topped boulder polished smooth by generations of laid-out wares—serves as the village's market, where the carvers display their rune-wands, naming-runes, and protective wards for the traders and petitioners who make the climb. Payment is never in silver alone: the carvers take iron, amber, true tales, and, for the greatest work, a sworn oath, and the traders who come up the road are a specialized breed—tough, laconic, and unfailingly respectful of the stones and the carvers, for a man who insults the Rune-Guild does not get a second carven word from them. The road is closed by snow for half the year, and in that time the village is alone with the moor and the stones and the fog.
