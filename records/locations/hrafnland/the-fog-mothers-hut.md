Set apart from the bothy-ring, on a low mound at the moor's edge where the fog gathers first and lingers longest, the Fog-Mother's Hut is the dwelling and seidr-ground of the Volva Aldís Fog-Mother, [[location:hra-runarholm|Rúnarhólm]]'s seidr-woman and the village's co-ruler—a low, round, turf-roofed hut whose walls are of stacked stone and whose only door faces north, toward the ice, and is hung with a curtain of grey woven wool that is never fully still, even when the wind is calm. Inside, the hut is a single, dim, smoke-hazed room: a central hearth whose fire is never allowed to go out, a sleeping-platform covered in furs, and a seidr-platform of raised earth where Aldís sits on a whalebone seat—the high-seat—and enters the seidr-trance that lets her see what is hidden, what is coming, and what the stones have said. The air smells of woodsmoke, dried herbs—yarrow, mugwort, and the frostwort that grows only on the moor—and the faint, cold, electric scent of active seidr. Aldís herself is small, old, and wrapped in layers of grey wool and fox-fur, her white hair braided with bone-beads and her eyes the pale, distant grey of the moor-fog she is named for. She speaks little, and what she speaks is delivered in a low, even voice that the villagers treat as law, for the Fog-Mother's word on a birth, a death, or a doom-rune is the word that the village lives by, and even the Master Carver, who rules the craft, defers to her in matters of the unseen.
