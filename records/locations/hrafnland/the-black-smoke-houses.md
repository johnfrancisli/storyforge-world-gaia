Clustered behind the headland's crest, where the wind carries the smoke away from the living-shanties, the Black-Smoke Houses are [[location:hra-hvalhofn|Hvalhöfn]]'s curing-works—a tight cluster of low, stone-walled, turf-roofed smoke-shacks whose chimneys trail a perpetual, dense, grey-black smoke that reeks of peat, blubber, and cured meat and that can be smelled from a mile downwind. Inside, the shacks are divided into hanging-chambers where strips of whale meat, slabs of seal, and the occasional walrus-side hang from rafters of whalebone and driftwood, cured slowly over smoldering peat fires tended by the station's curers—a dour, smoke-blackened crew who judge a cure by touch and smell and who would never eat meat they hadn't cured themselves. The floors are a greasy, ash-scattered mess, and the heat is dry, close, and eye-watering, but the output—dark, salt-rubbed, smoke-cured meat that can keep for two winters without spoiling—is [[location:hra-hvalhofn|Hvalhöfn]]'s second great export after oil, and the jarls of [[location:hrafnland|Hrafnland]] pay in iron and amber for the chests of black-smoke meat that ship out on every trader. The master curer, a silent, soot-stained man named Björn Ash-Hand, has cured meat on this headland for forty years and claims he can still taste the first whale he ever smoked.
