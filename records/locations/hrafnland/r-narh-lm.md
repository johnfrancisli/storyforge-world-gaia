On a wind-stripped moor where the frost heaves the earth into low, mossy mounds and a ring of standing stones juts from the bog like the broken teeth of a buried giant, Rúnarhólm is the rune-carvers' village of [[location:hrafnland|Hrafnland]]—a settlement of fewer than three hundred souls that is nonetheless revered across the nation as the keeper of its oldest memory. The village itself is a scatter of low, windowless stone bothies with turf roofs grown over with frost-tolerant heather, each built around a central hearth and sharing a wall with the next, so that the whole settlement forms a single wind-resistant ring with the standing stones at its heart. Every doorpost, lintel, and roof-beam is carved with runes—protective wards, lineage records, weather-prophecies, and the names of the dead—and the air carries the dry, mineral scent of freshly cut stone and the faint ozone tang of active seidr. The rune-carvers, men and women of the Rune-Guild, work the standing stones in slow rotation, recutting the ancient marks before the frost can wear them smooth, and their chanting carries across the moor at dawn and dusk in a low, droning harmony that the villagers say keeps the frost giants in their sleep. Few outsiders come to Rúnarhólm save those seeking a rune-wand, a naming-rune, or a doom-rune to lay on an enemy, and the carvers accept payment in iron, amber, or a true tale—never in mere silver.
