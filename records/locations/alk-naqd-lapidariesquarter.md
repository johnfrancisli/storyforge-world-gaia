The Lapidaries' Quarter is the workshop district of Naqd, a cluster of low stone buildings on the middle terrace where the rough stones from the mines are washed, sorted, cut, and polished by the lapidaries — the skilled craftsmen who are the mountain's true treasure, for without them the raw stone is worth a tenth of its finished value. The quarter is loud with the whine of the cutting-wheels, the hiss of water on hot stone, and the tap of the setters' hammers, and the air is thick with the fine stone-dust that coats everything in a pale film and that the lapidaries breathe all their lives, so that the old ones cough and spit coloured phlegm. Each workshop is a single room with a great horizontal cutting-wheel turned by a mule or a water-mill, fed with abrasive sand and water, and the lapidaries sit at the wheel for hours shaping each stone, their hands steady and their eyes sharp. The master lapidaries are the elite of the town, honoured by the Gem-Houses and paid in stone-shares, and their signatures are cut into the back of every fine stone. The apprentices, young and dust-covered, sleep in the workshops and dream of the day they will cut a perfect beryl.
