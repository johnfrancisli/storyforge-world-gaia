The Old Foundations are the residential quarter of the reclaimed city, a cluster of new thatched houses built on the stone foundations of the old city's dwellings along the streets radiating from the plaza. The old walls, knee-high and mossy, serve as the base for new wooden walls and palm-thatch roofs, and each house has a small cleared yard where cassava, peppers, and medicinal herbs grow in the rich dark soil that has accumulated over centuries. The streets between them are the old limestone paving, cracked and root-heaved but walkable, and water runs along the old drainage channels when it rains. It is a quiet neighbourhood of families — the shamans, scholars, labourers, and hunters who make up the settlement — and smoke rises from the cookfires morning and evening, carrying the smell of roasting cassava, fish-stew, and the bitter green tea brewed from yoco bark. Weavers work under the eaves in the daytime, and children run the old streets calling to each other. A communal fermenting-house at the street's end produces the sour cassava beer that is shared at every gathering, its smell sharp and yeasty on the afternoon air.
