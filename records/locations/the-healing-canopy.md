The Healing Canopy is the medicine-house of Motu Lotu, a large open-sided shelter of driftwood poles and a great sail-cloth awning stretched between the palms at the camp's center, where the camp's healers — a council of women and men from a dozen islands, each carrying the herbal and spiritual medicine of their home — tend the sick, the injured, and the grief-wounded who are the camp's particular affliction. The floor is sand covered with woven mats, and the space is divided by hung bark-cloth curtains into treatment-bays where the healers work: brewing herbal decoctions in clay pots on small hearths, setting broken bones with bamboo splints, suturing wounds with bone needles and fibre-thread, and — most often and most needed — sitting with the grief-stricken, the refugees who have lost homes and family to the sea, singing the comfort-songs and administering the calming brews that are the healers' particular skill. The air smells of green herbs, wood-smoke, the bitter tang of medicinal decoctions, and the clean salt of the sea-wind that flows through the open sides. Bundles of dried herbs hang from the awling-poles — gathered from the surviving islands and sent as aid — and a small spirit-corner holds offerings to the healing-spirits and the sea-spirit, whose anger the healers say must be acknowledged before grief can ease. The chief healer, a quiet woman from a drowned atoll, coordinates the council and trains apprentices from among the camp's youth.
