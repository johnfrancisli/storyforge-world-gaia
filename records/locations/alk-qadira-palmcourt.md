The Palm Court is the palace of the Water-Sheikh of Qadira, a low complex of white lime-washed buildings set within a walled garden of date-palms at the lake's edge, its walls so thick that the interior stays cool even at the desert's noon, and its roofs shaded by woven reed-mats on poles. The heart of the court is the Hall of Water, an open pavilion whose floor is a shallow marble basin fed by a channel from the lake, through which cool water flows continuously so that the Sheikh and his guests sit on silk cushions with their feet in the moving water while they conduct business. The smell is of water, green dates, and the attar of roses grown in the inner garden. The Sheikh's seat is a simple reed chair — by tradition no ruler of an oasis may sit on a throne, for all are equal before the water — and around it the clan-masters and the oasis elders sit on the floor to be heard. The court is busy at dawn and dusk when the water-rights are allocated, and quiet at midday when the whole city sleeps. Guards in loose white linen with silver-hilted knives stand at the gate, but the court is open to any who come in peace.
