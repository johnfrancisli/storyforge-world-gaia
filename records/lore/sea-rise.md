The Tide Archipelago is drowning, slowly, and everyone who lives there knows it. Over the last two generations the high-water mark has crept up the atoll walls until the lowest islands are gone entirely and their people have become refugees crowding onto the higher ones. The wayfinders read the changing currents; the tattoo-carvers now inscribe the maps of islands that no longer exist, so the drowned places are remembered on living skin. No one agrees on why the sea rises — the pearl-divers blame a slumbering sea-spirit, the chiefs call it the world simply changing — but the Council of Atolls must decide how many refugees the surviving islands can hold before the hosts drown alongside their guests.
