Across Gaia the unseen world is populated, and every nation sorts its spirits into a different order. Tsukuyomi classifies its yokai from the low mischievous tsukumogami up through the kitsune and yuki-onna to the great kami bound at the Grand Shrine, each governed by shrine-pacts of varying strictness. Valdris names dragons the highest of spirits and treats the lesser sylphs and undines of the mages' circle as minor things, though the elves of [[location:val-elfshade|Elfshade]] would argue the old forest-spirits outrank even wyrms. [[location:al-khayzar|Al-Khayzar]] binds djinn by true-name under the Pact, ranking them from the lesser ifrit-servants to the marid lords a binding family may call only once in a lifetime. [[location:verdania|Verdania]] speaks of a single Great Serpent at the peak of the hierarchy with the jaguar-spirit and river-spirits beneath it, all bound by pacts the shamans renew each generation. [[location:hrafnland|Hrafnland]] counts the frost-giants and the völur's wights as a folk apart from the living, neither worshipped nor bargained with but respected and warded against. [[location:tide-archipelago|The Tide Archipelago]] reveres the sea-spirits and the ancestor-ghosts of drowned atolls as one tangled hierarchy no carver has fully mapped. The one thing every nation agrees on: a spirit is not a god, and a pact is not a prayer — it is a contract, and contracts can be broken.
