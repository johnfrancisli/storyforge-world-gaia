Gaia's nations remember themselves in sound and image. Tsukuyomi plays the koto and shamisen in tea houses and shrine courtyards, paints ink scrolls and lacquerware, and tells its history through bunraku puppet-plays as often as through books. Valdris fills its cathedrals with the Dawnfire choir and its halls with the harp and fiddle of the old bards, while the elves of [[location:val-elfshade|Elfshade]] keep a quieter art — illuminated manuscripts and moonlit statuary in grove-stone. [[location:sangguo|Sangguo]] prizes the guqin and erhu, the ink-wash landscape, and the strategists' habit of teaching history as a verse a child can memorize. [[location:verdania|Verdania]] carves its stories into totem-poles and the tattooed skin of its people, and its music is the drum and the bone-flute calling the canopy together. [[location:hrafnland|Hrafnland]]'s skalds chant the sagas to the lyre beside the longhouse fire, and the rune-carvers cut the same stories into standing stone. [[location:al-khayzar|Al-Khayzar]] fills the night bazaar with the oud and the frame-drum, decorates its mosques and palaces in geometric tile and arabesque, and recites its genealogies as poetry that binding families can recite back ten generations. [[location:tide-archipelago|The Tide Archipelago]] makes music of the conch-shell horn and the slit-drum, dances its histories in the haka, and weaves its stories into the mat-patterns no atoll weaves quite the same way.
