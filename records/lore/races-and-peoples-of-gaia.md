Gaia's peoples are not interchangeable silhouettes with fantasy labels. Their
visible anatomy is part of canon, but culture, clothing, complexion, build, and
individual features still come from each character record rather than from a
racial stereotype.

The river-born Mizuhito are amphibious humanoids whose blue-green colouring and
webbed hands and feet remain visible across the communities of
[[location:verdania|Verdania]]. Otter, serpent, bird, and jaguar beast-kin are
separate peoples; one animal lineage must never borrow another's ears, scales,
feathers, fur, or tail. Elves, dwarves, and halflings retain broadly humanlike
faces, but their pointed ears, compact builds, and scale of stature respectively
must read clearly in a group image.

Tsukuyomi's yokai often move through human society in concealed forms. Kitsune,
tanuki, tengu, oni, yuki-onna, rokurokubi, and nekomata therefore distinguish
between a natural or revealed form and an explicitly authored disguise. A
character's own appearance decides which signs are currently visible; the lore
reference supplies the anatomy the prompt must understand rather than forcing
every concealed tail, wing, horn, or elongated neck into every scene.

Frost giants must read at giant scale, not merely as pale humans. Djinn and
spirits must retain visible elemental or incorporeal evidence unless a character
record explicitly requests a fully convincing mortal disguise. In every case,
the individual character's visual fields outrank this population baseline.
