# The Culture of Tsukuyomi

The culture of [[location:tsukuyomi|Tsukuyomi]] is a delicate balance of intense martial discipline and profound spiritual sensitivity. It is a land where the boundary between the material world and the spirit realm is thin, and life is lived with an acute awareness of its own fleeting nature.

## Cultural Themes

*   **Impermanence and Natural Beauty**: Tsukuyomi culture finds the greatest beauty in things that do not last—the falling cherry blossom, the changing of the seasons. This concept of impermanence (mono no aware) colors their art and their worldview.
*   **Duty, Honor, and Martial Excellence**: The samurai class dictates the social ethos. Absolute loyalty to one's lord, mastery of the blade, and the willingness to die for honor are core tenets of their society.
*   **Connection to the Spirit World (Kami)**: Spirits, or Kami, reside in every tree, rock, and river. Acknowledging and appeasing these spirits is essential for a harmonious life.

## Social Practices

*   **Purification Rituals**: Before entering a shrine or undertaking a significant task, purification rituals are mandatory. Cleanliness of the body is closely tied to purity of the spirit.
*   **Absolute Loyalty (Bushido)**: The warrior code dictates a life of discipline and service. A failure of duty often demands extreme measures to restore honor.
*   **Vibrant Festivals**: Despite the strict social codes, festivals honoring the Kami are vibrant, lively affairs featuring masks, dances, and communal celebrations.

## Atmosphere

Tsukuyomi is disciplined, spiritually vibrant, and refined. It balances quiet, meditative contemplation in its gardens and shrines with the taut, deadly readiness of its martial class.
