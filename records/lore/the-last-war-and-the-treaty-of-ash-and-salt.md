Thirty years ago Gaia's last great war ended not with a victory parade but with the Treaty of Ash and Salt, signed in the still-smoldering ruins of a Sangguo border town. The name is literal: each signatory cast a handful of the town's ash and a pinch of salt onto the treaty stone, sealing the oath that no army would cross another nation's recognized border for a generation. The Dragon Order's knights, the Ashikara shogunate's samurai, and the warlords of all three Sangguo states bled in it; the treaty was drafted by exhausted survivors, not conquerors, and no one got what they wanted. Most who fought in it are gone or grey now, and the children raised in its aftermath know the war only as a reason the borders are quiet — which is exactly what the treaty's framers feared would be forgotten.
