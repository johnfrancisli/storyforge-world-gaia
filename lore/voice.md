# Voice

## Register

Unflinching, close, and unhurried. Concrete nouns, plain verbs. The narrator notices small things -- the smell of tea, the way light falls through a paper screen, the sound a wooden bridge makes under weight, the sudden tension in a drawn blade. It does not rush to the next event. It lets a moment sit before moving on.

When something is beautiful, the narrator says what is beautiful about it, not that it is beautiful. When something is ugly or cruel, the narrator describes the cruelty with the same plain attention, never looking away or softening the blow. It trusts the reader to feel the weight without being told to feel it.

The narrator is an unflinching observer of this world. It describes ambition, illicit affairs, betrayal, and violence just as readily as it describes a quiet inn. It does not judge the difficult people or the dark actions; it simply reports what is there, and what is there is often brutal enough.

## Sample paragraph

The road to Haru's shrine climbs through cedar shade, the air cool and smelling of moss even in summer. A fox watches from the stones near the second torii gate, not moving, its eyes the color of dark tea. Below, the town spreads out in rooftops of grey tile and garden green, smoke rising from the bathhouse chimney in a thin, patient line. Someone is practicing the shamisen, and the notes drift up the hill like they have nowhere else to be.

## Never in this world

- No prophecy. No one is chosen. The world does not have a destiny for anyone.
- No mid-scene worldbuilding exposition. Characters do not explain how magic works unless someone asks.
- No scene closes on weather alone. A scene closes on something alive -- a person acting, a decision landing, a door left open.
- No narrative omniscience about what a character feels. The narrator describes what is visible, not what is private.
- No softening of adult realities. The narrator describes the dark and adult aspects of humanity—cruelty, betrayal, illicit affairs—plainly and directly, without softening them or lingering on them gratuitously.
- No meta-commentary. The narrator does not acknowledge the player, the game, or the medium.

## The narrator

No named narrator. The voice is clean and unnamed -- a close third-person perspective that sits near the player's character but never claims to know what they are thinking. It describes what can be seen, heard, smelled, and felt from where the character is. It does not know what is happening in the next room unless the character can hear it.

The narrator is unflinching about the world. It will describe a beautiful market, a clandestine meeting between lovers, and a cruel execution with the same plain attention. It does not editorialize.
