# The Heavenly Realm

Above Gaia, above the clouds and the sky that anyone can see, there is a place that is sometimes called the Heavenly Realm. It is not heaven in the religious sense -- it is not a reward, not a paradise, not an afterlife. It is a place where the world begins its stories.

## What it is

[[location:heavenly-realm|The Heavenly Realm]] is a quiet, luminous place of floating islands, gentle mist, and gardens that bloom in every season at once. It is not infinite. It is not crowded. It is mostly empty, and that emptiness is intentional. This is a place of choosing, not of living.

A pavilion stands at its center, open on all sides, with a view that looks down through layers of cloud to the world below. From there you can see all seven nations spread out like a map -- the green of Valdris, the island-dotted blue of the Tide Archipelago, the brown and gold of [[location:sangguo|Sangguo]]'s river valleys, the deep green of [[location:verdania|Verdania]], the grey-white of [[location:hrafnland|Hrafnland]]'s fjords, the amber of [[location:al-khayzar|Al-Khayzar]]'s deserts, the misted green of Tsukuyomi's mountains.

A figure waits there. Not a god -- a guide. They are old, or they seem old, and they have been here longer than anyone currently alive in Gaia. They do not judge. They do not command. They ask one question: where do you want to begin?

## What happens here

[[location:heavenly-realm|The Heavenly Realm]] is where a soul new to Gaia arrives. Not every person in Gaia passes through here -- most are born to parents, live their lives, and never know this place exists. But some arrive. The reincarnated, the displaced, the ones who come from elsewhere. They wake here, in the garden, with fragmented memories of another life and no clear understanding of how they got here.

The guide offers them a choice. Not a grand destiny -- a starting point. Which nation, which life, which beginning. The choice is real, and it is permanent. Once you walk down from the Heavenly Realm into one of the seven nations, you cannot come back here to choose again.

The guide does not explain the nations in detail. They give a sentence or two about each -- enough to recognize, not enough to decide well. The decision is meant to be made partly blind. That is the point.

## What the guide is

The guide is not a god. They do not claim to be. They are something older than the current arrangement of things -- a remnant of whatever built the Old Roads, perhaps, or a part of the world that was left behind when the rest took shape. They are kind, but not warm. They are patient, but not parental. They do their work and they do not explain why.

The guide does not know everything. They know the nations of Gaia in general terms -- enough to describe each one in a sentence. They do not know what is happening right now in any of them. They do not know the future. They are as surprised by what the reincarnated become as anyone else.

## Why this is the opening

The player begins in the Heavenly Realm. They arrive with no body, no name, no history in this world. The guide speaks to them, describes the seven nations, and asks them to choose. The choice determines where the first turn of the story takes place and which nation's situation they land in the middle of.

This is not a character creation screen. It is a scene. The guide is a character with their own voice and their own limits. The player's choice is a real choice, made in-character, with incomplete information. The guide will answer questions but will not give a full briefing. They will not tell you which nation is safest or which has the most interesting problems. They will tell you what each place is like, in the broadest strokes, and let you decide.
