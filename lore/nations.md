# Nations of Gaia

Seven nations, each a world unto itself, connected by sea routes, mountain passes, and the broken fragments of the Old Roads. They share a planet and a history they mostly disagree about.

---

## Tsukuyomi Shogunate

Edo-period Japan reimagined. A land of shrine-towns, hot-spring villages, terraced rice valleys, and castle-cities held by samurai clans. The supernatural is woven into daily life -- fox spirits run message shops, tengu teach swordsmanship in mountain dojos, and the shrine network maintains the wards that keep the wilder yokai at a distance from settled areas.

The population is primarily human, with native yokai communities (kitsune, tengu, oni, nekomata, tanuki, rokurokubi, and yuki-onna) living in open or concealed forms. Tsukuyomi has no native elven population; outsiders encountered here are occasional foreign travelers, scholars like [[character:layla-al-falaki|Layla al-Falaki]] calibrating star charts at mountain shrines, or seductive demon tricksters like [[character:lilise-the-alluring|Lilise the Alluring]] operating in the capital's shadow quarters.

The Shogunate is nominally unified under the Ashikara shogunate, but the clans hold real power in their territories. The current shogun is elderly and the succession is unspoken. Peace holds, but the clans watch each other.

Magic: **Shrine magic** -- ritual, precise, and tied to the network of shrines that covers the land. Miko (shrine priestesses) and kannushi (priests) channel spirit-energy through ceremony, ofuda talismans, and purification rites. It is methodical work -- the right words, the right gestures, the right time of day. A shrine keeper who neglects their rounds finds their ward weakening. Power scales with devotion and precision, not talent.

Key places: [[location:tsuki-no-miyako|Tsuki-no-Miyako]] (the capital, seat of the shogun), [[location:haru-shrine-town|Haru's shrine town]] (a mountain hot-spring village), the Cedar Road (the main pilgrimage route).

Feel: Warm, ritual, beautiful. The smell of cedar and incense. Paper screens and wooden floors. The sound of water.

## Valdris

Medieval Europe meets Lord of the Rings. A kingdom of stone castles, green hills, deep forests, and mountain passes where dragons roost. Humans, elves, dwarves, and halflings live here in an uneasy alliance held together by old oaths and the memory of worse times.

The [[location:valdris|Kingdom of Valdris]] is the oldest continuous government in Gaia, and it knows it. The dragon-bonded knights -- once the kingdom's strongest defenders -- have not received a new dragon bond in a generation. In the high crags above [[location:dragonrest|Dragonrest]], the mystery of **The Silence of the Drakes** hangs heavy: all male dragon-kin vanished over a century ago, leaving only solitary females like [[character:valkyria-ashscale|Valkyria Ashscale]] guarding the ancient wyrm-carved sanctuaries. Meanwhile, tournament grounds in the capital are occasionally shaken by unarmored demon duelists like [[character:barash-the-cleaver|Barash the Cleaver]], while court enchantresses like [[character:velura-the-whisper|Velura the Whisper]] spin subtle webs behind noble tapestries.

Magic: **Dragon-bonded oaths** -- a knight swears an oath before a dragon, and if the dragon accepts, the knight gains a measure of the dragon's power: fire resistance, enhanced strength, longevity. The bond is personal and lifelong. If the dragon dies, the knight is diminished but not broken. If the knight breaks the oath, they lose everything. No new bonds have been made in twenty years. The dragons are not refusing -- they are simply not answering.

Key places: [[location:crownhaven|Crownhaven]] (the capital, a city of white stone and seven towers), [[location:dragonrest|Dragonrest]] (the mountain fortress of the old knightly order), Greywood (a forest village on the trade road).

Feel: Grand, storied, autumnal. Stone and banners. The sound of hooves on a bridge. Wind in the high towers.

## Sangguo

Ancient China reimagined through Romance of the Three Kingdoms. A vast land of river valleys, terraced mountains, silk-road trade, and walled cities, currently held in an uneasy truce between three states: Jin in the north, Chu in the south, and Wei in the west. The truce is three years old and counting.

Sangguo is predominantly human, and the imperial courts have subtly pursued a "human-only" state ideology. While amphibious Mizuhito still pilot canal locks, non-human artisans—especially master dwarven engineers like [[character:tie-luo|Tie Luo]] and stonemasons like [[character:shi-bao|Shi Bao]]—find themselves quietly excluded from state bureaus, forcing them into clandestine workshops or operating under human guild fronts. Sangguo has no domestic elven nobility or key figures; elves seen in the cities are visiting scholars like [[character:gwendolyn-of-val-hall|Gwendolyn of Val-Hall]]. High in the cloud-veiled peaks, solitary jade dragon ascetics like [[character:long-meihua|Long Meihua]] cultivate celestial breath alone, while demon martial challengers like [[character:azgash-ironfist|Azgash Ironfist]] seek out chi masters, and beguiling songstresses like [[character:xian-ya-the-foxdevil|Xian Ya]] outwit tax magistrates along the pleasure barges.

Magic: **Chi cultivation and spirit-pacts** -- internal energy cultivated through discipline, meditation, and forms. Masters can reinforce their bodies, move faster than the eye follows, and project force. Spirit-pacts are separate: agreements with qilin, dragon spirits, and ancestor spirits who offer counsel or power in exchange for service or tribute. The two are complementary but distinct.

Key places: [[location:jin-an|Jin'an]] (the northern capital, largest city in Gaia), the Chu Riverlands (a network of towns and rice paddies), the Long Pass (the mountain road between Jin and Wei).

Feel: Vast, political, elegant. Silk and lacquer. The sound of a guqin at night. Ink on rice paper.

## Verdania

Amazonian South America reimagined. A vast rainforest threaded by rivers, where stone cities are built into the canopy and the jungle is alive in ways that go beyond metaphor. The people of [[location:verdania|Verdania]] -- humans, amphibious Mizuhito river-folk, diverse beast-kin clans (bird, jaguar, serpent, and otter), and agile Cat-folk hunters and guides -- live in a relationship with the forest that is part reverence, part negotiation.

Deep in the sacred canopy of the Green Cathedral, secluded enclaves of ethereal, beautiful **Forest Elves** led by elder botanists like [[character:lyra-moonleaf|Lyra Moonleaf]] and spirit-weavers like [[character:sylva-orchid|Sylva Orchid]] guard ancient ironwood boughs in close alliance with river shamans like [[character:nara|Nara]]. Along the riverways, agile Cat-folk pathfinders navigate rope bridges, while wild demon sirens like [[character:morrigan-the-wild|Morrigan the Wild]] lure exploitative timber syndicates into bottomless mangrove bogs.

Verdania has no single government. It is a network of tribes and city-states connected by river trade and shared spiritual tradition. The temple cities of the old civilization -- built before anyone can remember -- stand deep in the jungle, half-reclaimed, still humming with power.

Magic: **Shamanic spirit-pacts** -- shamans negotiate with the spirits of river, tree, stone, and beast. A pact is a relationship, not a spell. The spirit agrees to help, and the shaman agrees to maintain the relationship -- offerings, songs, respect. Break the relationship and the spirit withdraws. The strongest shamans have pacts with ancient spirits that have been in their families for generations.

Key places: [[location:iara|Iara]] (the river capital, built on stilts over a great river), [[location:ver-altacanopy|Alta Canopy]] (the great tree-city suspended in the kapok crowns), the Green Cathedral (a temple city in the deep jungle), the Mist Falls (a trading post at the jungle's edge).

Feel: Lush, alive, humid. The sound of water and birds. Green light through leaves. Painted skin and featherwork.

## Hrafnland

Norse and Viking reimagined. A land of fjords, longships, sea-sagas, and the long dark of winter. The people are traders, raiders, and storytellers, and they believe a life worth living is one worth singing about.

The population is predominantly Norse human, with an established and respected elven minority settled along the fjord homesteads and smithies. In the deep crevasses of the north, solitary frost dragon-kin like [[character:vildis-frostscale|Vildis Frostscale]] wander the frozen cairns of their vanished male kin, while solitary Frost Giants dwell in the peaks. In trading hubs like [[location:skaldvik|Skaldvik]], horned demon duelists like [[character:morvath-the-unbeaten|Morvath the Unbeaten]] challenge Althing hearthguards to test mortal mettle, while foreign master smiths like [[character:takahashi-nobu|Takahashi Nobu]] exchange forge secrets at the anvil.

Hrafnland has no king. Each fjord is ruled by a jarl, and the jarls meet once a year at the Althing to settle disputes and make laws. It works most of the time.

Magic: **Runic seidr** -- power carved into wood, bone, and stone. Each rune is a word in a language older than people. A rune-carver learns the words and their meanings, and carving them correctly channels power: protection, fire, insight, wind. Seidr also includes prophecy-visions and spirit-work, practiced mostly by the volur (wise women). It is respected and slightly feared.

Key places: [[location:skaldvik|Skaldvik]] (the largest trading port), Frosthollow (a fjord village), the Althing Mound (the annual meeting place).

Feel: Cold, salt, wood-smoke. The creak of a longship. Aurora over black water. Mead and saga.

## Al-Khayzar

Arabian Nights and Persian reimagined. A land of desert city-states, spice markets, oasis gardens, and starlit caravanserais. Djinn walk the markets in human form, and the oldest families carry binding contracts with them that date back centuries.

The population is largely human with an integrated desert elven craft community specializing in calligraphy, glassmaking, and fine botanical dyes. Trade along the Old Road brings intercultural travelers from afar, from northern ivory merchants like [[character:einar-saltstride|Einar Saltstride]] to rainforest healers like [[character:iara-blossom|Iara Blossom]]. In the starlit alleys of the Star Bazaar, silk-veiled demon enchantresses like [[character:qamara-the-beguiling|Qamara the Beguiling]] weave labyrinthine contracts that outwit haughty guildmasters.

Al-Khayzar is a confederation of city-states bound by trade and the Pact of the Lamp -- an agreement that no city will war upon another within the confederation. The pact holds because trade is more profitable than war, and because the djinn who enforce it are older and more patient than any dynasty.

Magic: **Djinn-binding** -- binding contracts with djinn, the fire-spirits of the desert. A binder negotiates a contract: the djinn provides power or service, and the binder provides something in return -- years of their life, a promise, a secret, a firstborn. The contracts are ironclad and supernatural. Break one and the djinn collects, immediately and without mercy. The most powerful binders carry contracts that have been in their families for generations. Djinn are not servants. They are partners who are always counting.

Key places: [[location:zaffar|Zaffar]] (the spice capital, a city of domes and minarets), the Mirror Oasis (a waystation on the deep desert route), the Star Bazaar (a night market that opens only after sundown).

Feel: Golden, warm, perfumed. The sound of water in a fountain. Spice and cardamom. Stars over sand.

## The Tide Archipelago

Polynesian and Southeast Asian reimagined. A warm-water world of hundreds of islands, big and small, connected by wayfinding and song. The voyagers of the Archipelago are the greatest navigators in Gaia, reading stars, currents, and birds across open water that would drown anyone else.

The islands are home to seafaring humans and agile, quick-footed **Cat-folk** who excel as pearl divers, scouts, and reef navigators. In the remote basalt spires near [[location:tide-motu-peleki|Motu Peleki]], solitary sapphire dragon-kin like [[character:kaimana-deepscale|Kaimana Deepscale]] guard ancient sunken wyrm-shrines. In the bustling anchorages of [[location:lani-atoll|Lani Atoll]], foreign hydraulicians like [[character:feng-yulan|Feng Yulan]] engineer coral breakwaters against rising tides, while haven demon sirens like [[character:malyssa-the-siren|Malyssa the Siren]] play high-stakes dice with privateer captains.

The Archipelago has no central authority. Each island has its own chief or council, and disputes are settled by the wayfinder guilds -- the navigators who are also the messengers, diplomats, and historians of the sea. The rising sea levels are putting pressure on everyone.

Magic: **Wayfinding song-magic** -- songs that carry power across water. A wayfinder's song can calm a storm, call a wind, speak to a whale, or send a message to another island. The songs are learned by ear and passed from teacher to student across generations. They only work over open water. A song sung inland is just a song.

Key places: [[location:lani-atoll|Lani Atoll]] (the central gathering island), the Singing Reef (a sacred navigation point), [[location:tide-motu-peleki|Motu Peleki]] (the volcanic fire-mountain island, home to the fire-spirit Peleki).

Feel: Warm, salt, open. The sound of waves on a reef. Stars and warm wind. Tattoo patterns and carved wood.
