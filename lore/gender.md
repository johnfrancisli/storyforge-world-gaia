# Gender in Gaia

## The two genders

Gaia recognises **two genders**: woman and man.

- **Woman** — `gender: woman`
- **Man** — `gender: man`

Every character record carries one of these. There are no additional values,
no third standings, and no cultural exceptions. The narrator and the text
generator need a simple, reliable answer, and two genders provide that.

## What the field means

The `gender:` field on a character record is the character's gender — full
stop. It is not a pronoun preference, not a social role separate from gender,
and not something that changes by nation. A woman is a woman in every nation
she walks into, and the same for a man.

## Attraction, partnership, and orientation

**Who a person is attracted to is separate from their gender.** A woman may
be attracted to women, to men, or to both. The same is true for men. Gaia
does not label or categorise orientation on character records — it is part of
a character's personality and story, not a structural field.

Homosexual, bisexual, and other pairings are accepted in most of Gaia, but
**not everywhere**. Four nations enforce laws or customs against same-gender
pairings, and the severity varies sharply. This is one of the clearest
cultural fault lines a traveller crosses.

**Where it is punishable by death:**

- **[[location:sangguo|Sangguo]]** — The Confucian-derived legal code treats
  same-gender union as a disruption of family lineage obligation. The
  magistrate's yamen may sentence both parties to death, though enforcement
  is uneven — wealthy families sometimes buy silence, and the law falls
  hardest on those without protectors.
- **[[location:al-khayzar|Al-Khayzar]]** — The Binding Families' moral code,
  reinforced by djinn-contract tradition, holds that a person's binding
  obligation is to produce heirs for the family line. Same-gender
  relationships are treated as contract fraud against the family. Sentences
  are public and brutal, intended as a warning.

**Where it is punishable by prison:**

- **[[location:valdris|Valdris]]** — The noble houses of Valdris trace
  bloodlines through marriage alliances, and a same-gender union that
  produces no heirs is treated as a breach of dynastic duty. Prison
  sentences are typically imposed on commoners; nobles face quieter
  sanctions — disinheritance, forced marriage, or exile to a distant
  posting. The law exists, but the powerful rarely meet it.

**Where it is not illegal but not accepted:**

- **[[location:tsukuyomi|Tsukuyomi]]** — There is no statute against
  same-gender partnership, but the shrine culture and the samurai
  households treat it as a failure of social duty. A same-gender couple
  in Tsukuyomi faces quiet exclusion — denied patronage, passed over for
  appointment, cut from the network of obligation that makes life in the
  shogunate workable. No one is arrested. Everyone notices.

**Where it is free:**

In [[location:hrafnland|Hrafnland]], the
[[location:tide-archipelago|Tide Archipelago]],
[[location:verdania|Verdania]], and the free city of
[[location:zaffar|Zaffar]], same-gender pairings carry no legal or social
penalty. A couple is a couple, and what matters is whether they hold their
obligations — to their crew, their village, their trade, or their family —
not the gender of who they hold them with.

These differences are a source of story. A Hrafnlander who loves freely in
port and then sails to Sangguo carries something that could kill them. A
Valdrisan noble who loves someone of the same gender must choose between
honesty and inheritance. A Tsukuyomi shrine servant who falls for a
colleague loses nothing in law and everything in standing. The rules of the
nation are part of the pressure on the character, not a footnote.

What varies even in the accepting nations is the **custom** around
partnership — how a union is formalised, whether it involves families or
just individuals, what obligations it carries. The gender of the partners
does not change those customs; the customs apply to any couple.

## Cross-race attraction

Attraction between races is common and unremarkable. A human and an elf, a
cat-folk and a beast-kin, a dwarf and a river-folk — none of these pairings
raises eyebrows in any city large enough to have met a foreigner. The
[[lore:old-roads|Old Roads]] moved people as freely as goods for
centuries, and the mixing that resulted is part of Gaia's population.

See `lore/races.md` for the full account of how races relate and
reproduce.

## What this is not

This file does not describe biological sex in detail, family structure, or
inheritance law. Those belong in the nations' own lore, where they can differ
properly. What this file fixes is the **gender system**: two values, applied
everywhere, with no exceptions and no expansions.
