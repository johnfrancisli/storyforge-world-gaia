# Magic in Gaia

Magic is one force. It does not have a name. The people of Gaia do not agree on what it is, where it comes from, or how it works. They only agree that it exists, and that every nation has found a different way to speak to it.

No system is stronger than another. A shrine maiden from Tsukuyomi and a djinn-binder from [[location:al-khayzar|Al-Khayzar]] are not using different levels of the same thing -- they are using entirely different languages for something that does not care which language you use. What matters is fluency, practice, and the cost you are willing to pay.

## The Seven Traditions

### Shrine Magic (Tsukuyomi)
Ritual and precise. Power channeled through ceremony, ofuda talismans, and purification. The shrine network is the infrastructure -- each shrine maintains a ward, and the wards together form a barrier that keeps the wild yokai at a distance from settled areas. A shrine keeper who neglects their rounds weakens the ward. Power scales with devotion and precision.

**Cost:** Time and attention. Shrine magic is daily work -- the rituals must be performed correctly and on schedule. A powerful shrine maiden is one who has spent years doing the same thing perfectly.

### Dragon-Bonded Oaths (Valdris)
A knight swears an oath before a dragon. If the dragon accepts, the knight gains a share of the dragon's power: fire resistance, enhanced strength, longevity. The bond is personal, lifelong, and unbreakable without cost. No new bonds have been made in twenty years.

**Cost:** The oath itself. A bonded knight is bound to the terms of their oath for life. Break it and the power is gone, sometimes violently. The dragon decides the terms, and dragons are not generous.

### Chi Cultivation and Spirit-Pacts (Sangguo)
Two complementary paths. Chi cultivation is internal -- energy built through discipline, meditation, and martial forms. Masters reinforce their bodies, move with superhuman speed, and project force. Spirit-pacts are external -- agreements with qilin, dragon spirits, and ancestor spirits who offer counsel or power in exchange for service or tribute.

**Cost:** Chi cultivation costs years of discipline and daily practice. Spirit-pacts cost whatever the spirit asks for, and spirits are specific in their demands.

### Shamanic Spirit-Pacts (Verdania)
Relationship-based magic. Shamans negotiate with the spirits of river, tree, stone, and beast. A pact is not a spell -- it is a relationship maintained through offerings, songs, and respect. The strongest shamans carry pacts that have been in their families for generations.

**Cost:** The relationship. Neglect the spirit and it withdraws. Offend it and it may turn against you. There is no shortcut and no substitution.

### Runic Seidr (Hrafnland)
Power carved into wood, bone, and stone. Each rune is a word in an old language. Carving them correctly channels power: protection, fire, insight, wind, death. Seidr also includes prophecy-visions and spirit-work, practiced mostly by the volur.

**Cost:** The carving takes time and material, and a rune carved wrong can backfire. Seidr visions are draining and sometimes show things the seer did not want to see. The runes do not care about your intentions.

### Djinn-Binding (Al-Khayzar)
Contracts with djinn -- fire-spirits of the desert. A binder negotiates a contract: the djinn provides power or service, and the binder provides something in return. Years of life, a promise, a secret, a firstborn. The contracts are ironclad and supernatural.

**Cost:** Whatever the contract specifies. Break one and the djinn collects immediately. The most powerful binders carry contracts that have been in their families for generations, and the djinn have been counting the interest.

### Wayfinding Song-Magic (Tide Archipelago)
Songs that carry power across water. A wayfinder's song can calm a storm, call a wind, speak to a whale, or send a message to another island. The songs are learned by ear and passed from teacher to student. They only work over open water.

**Cost:** The song must be sung correctly and with genuine intent. A wayfinder who has lost their voice cannot work magic. A wayfinder who does not believe their own song finds it does not work.

## The Old Roads

Not magic in the same sense, but related. [[lore:old-roads|The Old Roads]] are spirit-paths that once connected every nation in Gaia. Walking a working stretch can take you from Tsukuyomi to [[location:al-khayzar|Al-Khayzar]] in an afternoon. Most are broken. Some are lost. A few are deliberately kept shut by the people who live near them, because the Roads do not check who is using them.

The Roads are not gates or portals. They are paths -- you walk them, and the distance is shorter than it should be. The entrance is the hard part. Most entrances are hidden, overgrown, or sealed. Finding one is valuable. Using one is noticeable -- the Roads leave a trace that certain people can follow.
